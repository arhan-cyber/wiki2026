Step 6. Process level

In brief

In simulation, methyl myristate keeps a 1,200 W server below 85 °C at about a third of the flow of two commercial coolants. In use it emits 17 % less CO₂-eq per server-year; making it emits far more per kilogram.

This step connects the strain to its intended use as an immersion coolant. The full analyses are on the CFD and LCA pages; the results are summarised here.

Table 1. Process-level results for methyl myristate (MM) against an ester-based and a mineral-oil-based coolant.

Quantity Methyl myristate Ester-based Mineral-oil-based
Hottest server part at equal flow, 0.42 m/s (°C) 78.1 88.0 87.2
Flow needed to stay at or below 85 °C (m/s) 0.27 0.80 0.80
Pump power at that flow (W) 6.1 158.9 158.8
CO₂-eq per server-year, chiller (kg) 5,012 6,071 6,055
Production footprint (kg CO₂-eq per kg of coolant) 449 5.25 about 1.5 (screening value)

Computational fluid dynamics. Three-dimensional conjugate heat-transfer simulations in OpenFOAM (19 runs) compared the coolants for a 1,200 W server in one tank geometry. Methyl myristate meets the 85 °C limit at 0.27 m/s; the other coolants need 0.80 m/s. Details are on the CFD page.

Life cycle assessment. The screening study counts raw materials to factory gate, plus cooling electricity in use. Methyl myristate emits 17 % less CO₂-eq per server-year than the ester-based coolant on the India grid factor (about 1.6 kg CO₂-eq per kWh), and the extra production CO₂ is paid back in about 2.9 years. Making 1 kg emits 449 kg CO₂-eq (632 in the first process design) against about 5 for ester-based coolants. The study has not been critically reviewed under ISO 14044. Details are on the LCA page.
