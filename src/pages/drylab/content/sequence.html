Step 1. Residue correspondence between YlFAS and ScFAS2

In brief

A published mutation in a related yeast (Yarrowia lipolytica) changes the length of the fatty acids it makes. Aligning the two proteins’ sequences showed that the matching position in baker’s yeast is V1254, so V1254W is the candidate mutation. That the same change works in baker’s yeast is the assumption tested by the wet-lab construct.

Question. Which residue of Saccharomyces cerevisiae fatty acid synthase subunit 2 (ScFAS2) occupies the position of I1220 in the Yarrowia lipolytica enzyme (YlFAS)? Rigouin et al. identified I1220 in the fatty acid binding site of YlFAS as a determinant of chain length; the I1220W substitution increased C14 to 11.6 % of total fatty acids.1

Method. The YlFAS α chain (UniProt Q6CE16, 1,850 aa) was aligned globally with ScFAS2 (UniProt P19097, 1,887 aa) using EMBOSS Needle23 (BLOSUM62, gap opening penalty 10, gap extension penalty 0.5). A global alignment was chosen because the sequences differ in length and insertions shift the residue numbering.

Correction of an earlier accession. An earlier version of this page gave the Yarrowia accession as Q6C8H7 (1,852 aa). UniProt lists Q6C8H7 as a magnesium transporter (LPE10, 455 aa), not fatty acid synthase; the FAS α subunit is Q6CE16 (YALI0_B19382g, 1,850 aa; checked against the UniProt record on 17 October 2026). The alignment was repeated on 17 October 2026 with Q6CE16 and the same parameters (Biopython PairwiseAligner, BLOSUM62, gap open 10, extend 0.5, end gaps free) because the web Needle run was not archived. Result: score 6,267.5; 1,198 identical residues in 1,902 alignment columns (63.0 % identity, 77.9 % similarity, 67 gap columns). The three equivalences and the offset of 34 in Table 1 were reproduced unchanged. In other parts of the KS region the offset is 34 or 35, because of short gaps. The alignment file is held in the team’s project records.

Aligned positions

In brief

Three positions line up between the two enzymes with a constant offset of 34 residues and no gaps beside the key position. The proposed ScFAS2 counterpart of the Yarrowia mutation is V1254W.

The 21-residue window is identical at 19 positions (V/I and S/T differ), so the register is not in doubt over this stretch. This is one alignment with one algorithm; an independent multiple-sequence alignment of fungal FAS2 sequences, or a BLAST search, would extend this.

The three pairs are separated by a constant offset of 34 residues, which arises from insertions in ScFAS2 upstream of the ketoacyl synthase (KS) domain. No gaps occur at or beside the key column, so the V1254 ↔ I1220 equivalence does not depend on gap placement. The direct ScFAS2 analogue of the I1220W substitution is therefore V1254W. Transfer of the effect between species is by homology; its occurrence in S. cerevisiae is assumption A1, tested by the wet-lab construct.

Basis for ligand placement and for exclusion criteria

In brief

The published approach placed a fatty acid in the enzyme by copying it from a bacterial structure. The available yeast structure has no fatty acid bound, so placement had to be predicted. Positions were excluded for catalytic or mechanistic reasons, not because they are conserved.

Ligand placement in the published approach. As understood from the team’s reading of Rigouin et al.,1 the Yarrowia KS domain was modelled from three structures (PDB 2PFF, 2VKZ and 4V58), superimposed on Escherichia coli β-ketoacyl-ACP synthase I (KAS I) with bound capric acid (PDB 1F91), the C10 chain was copied into the equivalent pocket and extended to C16. Only the abstract of this paper was checked against the source; the structure list, the superposition and the C10-to-C16 extension are not independently verified. The starting structure used here (PDB 8PRW, resolution 1.9 Å) has no fatty acid in the KS3 pocket, so ligand placement had to be predicted (Step 2). [literature, partly unverified]

Conservation and necessity. A conservation filter would remove positions at which mutation is likely to be destructive. The three residues considered here are conserved because they set chain length, a property that every fatty-acid-producing organism requires; conservation score alone is therefore not a suitable exclusion criterion. The criterion applied was catalytic or mechanistic necessity.

Status of this step

In brief

Two analyses are not finished: a further sequence search and an analysis of which regions of the protein should not be changed. Finding more mutation sites is blocked until the second is done.

Natural extensions: a BLAST search of the template against ScFAS2, an independent multiple-sequence alignment as a robustness check on Table 1, and a conservation and phylogenetic analysis to identify segments that should not be mutated. Identification of further mutation sites depends on the latter. None of these was done by 17 October 2026.
