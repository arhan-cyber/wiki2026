Step 2. Ligand placement in the ketoacyl synthase domain

In brief

A computer model (Boltz-2) was used to predict where myristic acid, the 14-carbon fatty acid, sits in the active region of the yeast fatty acid synthase. The predicted position depended on how the calculation was set up, so it is not treated as a finding about the real enzyme.

Question. Does the ketoacyl synthase (KS) pocket accommodate a C14 acyl chain in silico, and which residues should be excluded from mutagenesis?

Answer. The data do not answer either part. Boltz-2 can place a C14 chain near the catalytic cysteine C1305 under a soft restraint, and also without one in a rerun, but it places caffeine, glucose and C8 acid there with equal or higher confidence, the apo cavity at C1305 (about 120–215 Å³) is far smaller than a C14 chain, and a change of random seed moves the ligand as much as the substitutions tested. The step therefore gives pose descriptions that depend on the setup, not evidence that the pocket accommodates C14. The residue list is a literature-based precaution (Table 4) that this step did not test. The pipeline has no passed positive control (an alignment-based Boltz-2 run on the 1F91 complex was planned and not run; a single-sequence version did not recover the crystal pose).

Setup. Boltz-21 was applied to the KS domain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae fatty acid synthase subunit 2 (ScFAS2; residues 1123–1657, UniProt annotation). Initial runs used Google Colab (T4 GPU) with --use_msa_server --diffusion_samples 1. The ligand was myristic acid (SMILES CCCCCCCCCCCCCC(=O)O).

Ligand placement without distance constraints was setup-dependent. In the initial run (single sample, no constraints), Boltz-2 positioned myristic acid away from the pocket (baseline ligand ipTM 0.511; interface position error 9.1 Å). In a repeat run on the laboratory server (five samples, fixed alignment), the ligand was placed 3.0–4.6 Å from the catalytic cysteine C1305 without constraints (ligand ipTM 0.80). Failure of Boltz-2 without constraints is therefore not claimed; the outcome depended on the setup. Subsequent runs used explicit distance constraints.

First-round (Colab) models. Tables 1 and 2 record the first round (Google Colab, one sample per run). They are kept for transparency and are not used as evidence. They cannot be fully traced: the output files of this round are not in the archive used for this page, the model names in Table 1 (scfas2_ks3_c14_v2, run3) map only to the ‘v2’ and ‘Run3’ rows of Table 2, the constraint of Run1 is recorded only as ‘none on max_dist’ (its meaning is not recoverable), and the criterion for selecting Run3 was not recorded (it has the highest ligand ipTM of the three Colab rows, which may have been the reason, but this is an inference). The C16 and C22 results of this round are not available. The reproducible record starts with the laboratory-GPU reruns below.

Residues examined in the acyl tunnel

In brief

Three residues were checked in every constrained model. One of them, V1254, was kept as the proposed mutation site (V1254W), which is an assumption by homology. M1251 was deprioritised for mutagenesis as a precaution.

All three residues have an equivalent in Yarrowia lipolytica FAS (YlFAS) and were examined in every constrained model (Table 3). In brief

The reported M1251 clash was not reproduced

In brief

The first runs suggested that the fatty-acid chain collides with a residue called M1251. Repeat runs did not show this collision, so the idea remains an untested hypothesis.

Untested interpretation: in the first constrained runs, the acyl chain overlapped M1251 at a distance of 0.96–1.77 Å, and the overlap was also present in the raw structural superposition constructed from 8PRW. In the reruns (next section) no model showed the overlap. The superposition is the remaining evidence that a closed gate is incompatible with the chain. The raw superposition is not reproduced as a figure on this page, so a reader cannot inspect the remaining evidence; this is a gap. Attempted remedies in the first round did not resolve the overlap: OpenBabel UFF minimisation, PyMOL sculpting, and the PyMOL mutagenesis wizard (which ranks pre-enumerated rotamers only and cannot identify an intermediate side-chain geometry).

Working interpretation. M1251 is described as a conformational gate in the cerulenin-bound structure4, which is the basis for deprioritising it as a mutation site (precautionary exclusion; mechanism unresolved). Whether a closed gate collides with a C14 chain in the ligand-bound state is not resolved by these data: the superposition suggests that it does, whereas the Boltz-2 reruns do not. Gate probes with open and closed templates and M1251A/W runs were planned and not run. The published G1250S-M1251W strain5 shows the position is mutable.

Reruns on the laboratory GPU server

In brief

The calculation was repeated 15 times on laboratory graphics processors in two rounds, giving 75 predicted structures. The ligand position changed with the constraints chosen, the earlier collision was not seen, and the confidence scores were uniformly high, so they could not separate the setups.

After the first runs, the experiment was repeated on three RTX A6000 GPUs with one setup throughout: the same 535-residue KS-domain input, Boltz-2 with five samples per run, seed 42, a fixed multiple-sequence alignment, and myristic acid (C14) unless stated. Two constraint designs were compared:

  • C1305 contact (soft cutoff). One pocket contact on the catalytic cysteine C1305 with a cutoff of 6, 8 or 10 Å, plus an unconstrained baseline, a V1254W variant, and C16 and C22 ligands at 8 Å (7 runs).
  • Forced gate contacts. Pocket contacts on M1251 and M1260 (and in one run also V1254) with force: true, with and without the Boltz steering potentials (8 runs).

In these two rounds, 75 models were generated. The earlier Colab runs used one sample and constraints that were not fully recorded, so they are not directly comparable. Figure A (top of the page) outlines the sequence of experiments and Figure B the two constraint designs. Figure 1 shows the distances obtained, Figure 2 the confidence scores and Figure 3 the comparison across chain lengths. Figure 4 shows one model as an example.

Three observations were made, with their limits:

  • The constraint design altered the pose. The distance to M1260 was 15–17 Å when only C1305 was constrained and 3.9–6.5 Å when M1260 was forced. Any statement of the form “C14 lies X Å from residue Y” therefore describes the setup as well as the pocket.
  • No clash with M1251 was observed in the reruns. M1251 remained 3.1–6.3 Å from the ligand in every model, and no ligand atom came closer than 2.4 Å to any protein atom, with or without steering potentials. The 0.96–1.77 Å values of the first runs were not reproduced, and the cause is unknown (software version, sample count, constraints and the distance measurement are candidate explanations that were not tested).
  • Confidence did not discriminate between setups. Overall confidence was 0.90–0.94 and ligand ipTM 0.74–0.88 for every run, including the unconstrained baseline.

Control batches: repeatability, seed variation, pocket mutation and decoy ligands

In brief

Ten further runs tested whether the model gave repeatable results, how much the result varied with the random seed, and whether it responded to changes in the pocket or in the ligand. Repeating a run with the same seed gave an identical result, but a different seed moved the ligand by as much as the amino-acid substitutions did, and decoy ligands were not separated by the confidence scores.

Two control batches of 30 minutes each were performed under the settings of the second round (soft contact on C1305 at 8 Å, five samples) with the binding-probability output of Boltz-2 enabled (Table 5). The first batch (four runs, 20 models) comprised a repeat of the wild-type C14 run (repeatability), a variant carrying four tryptophan substitutions in the pocket (M1251W, A1304W, A1348W and A1548W; sensitivity to pocket mutation), caffeine in place of myristic acid (decoy ligand), and V1254W. The second batch (six runs, 30 models) comprised the wild-type run with a different random seed (43 instead of 42; seed variation), M1251W, V1254A, C22 and C8 acids, and glucose (second decoy). Expected outcomes and thresholds were recorded and hashed before each batch (Table 6). The thresholds were provisional placeholders that the team never confirmed, so any later change to them would be post hoc; the results are reported as measurements, with the pre-registered criteria shown against them in Table 6 for transparency, not as formal pass or fail verdicts.

  • A repeat with the same seed was identical. For all five matched models, the ligand RMSD to the stored wild-type model was 0.00 Å and all distances were unchanged, so the stored numbers are a valid baseline for this setup.
  • A different seed changed the pose. Overall confidence (+0.004), ligand ipTM (+0.001) and the distance to C1305 (+0.06 Å) were stable.
  • The substitutions moved the ligand no more than the seed did. The displacement was 1.25 Å for the plug mutant, 2.11 Å for V1254A, 2.30 Å for V1254W and 2.95 Å for M1251W, against 3.41 Å between two seeds of the wild type.   • Decoy ligands were not separated. The caffeine, glucose and C8 models lay 8–11 Å from M1251 while satisfying the restrained contact with C1305 (2.8–3.2 Å). Under this setup the pose of the ligand varied between seeds by about as much as the substitutions tested, and differences of 1–2 Å between wild-type and mutant models are therefore not interpreted. The V1254W and V1254A results are reported as descriptions of the models and do not support or refute the proposed effect of the substitution. The weak response may reflect the contact restraint on C1305 or the unchanged alignment as well as the model itself; these factors were not separated. The binding-probability and affinity values are Boltz-2 scores and not binding constants.

Chain-length controls

In brief

Longer and shorter fatty acids were modelled as controls. A longer chain reached further into the pocket, as geometry predicts, but the distances do not show that the pocket prefers one chain length.

C8, C16 and C22 runs were included as controls: if the pocket discriminated on chain length, the ligands would be expected to behave differently within it. In the first round, the team’s notes record C14 at about 6 Å from the two target residues, whereas the C16 distances were non-uniform (about 5 Å and 8 Å); these first-round values cannot be re-measured (see above) and are not used as evidence. In the reruns with a soft contact on C1305, the C22 chain (and three of five C16 models) came within about 7 Å of M1260, whereas C14 remained about 15 Å away; with forced contacts the three ligands reached the same residues (Figure 3). A longer chain spans further, so these distances do not by themselves indicate a preference of the pocket for chain length. The unconstrained baseline also located the pocket and therefore does not serve as a negative control; a decoy ligand (caffeine and glucose), a repeat of the wild-type run, a second seed and a pocket-plug variant were used instead (previous section).

Limitations of this step

In brief

The results rest on one random seed per condition, on constraints that influence the pose, and on model-derived scores, so they indicate plausible geometry only.

  • No formal proximity criterion was defined. The cutoff was varied (6, 8 and 10 Å) and the spread across five models is reported, but no threshold was adopted.
  • Five samples drawn with one seed are not independent replicates. The spread across the five models describes the variability of the sampling; run-to-run variability was measured by one additional seed of the wild-type C14 condition only (Table 5).
  • The pose depended on the constraint design (Figure B); distances measured to residues that were themselves constrained partly reflect the constraint.
  • Free myristic acid was modelled in place of the covalent acyl-enzyme intermediate (assumption A3 on the model overview).
  • The binding-probability and affinity outputs of Boltz-2 are model-derived scores, not binding constants. The affinity module was developed for small-molecule binders in protein pockets, not for fatty acids that form a covalent acyl-enzyme thioester, so its output is outside its design scope and is not used as evidence.
  • There is no positive control for the Boltz-2 pipeline. The alignment-based Boltz-2 run on the E. coli KAS I complex 1F91 (planned in the pre-registration) was excluded because it needed the public E. coli sequence to be sent to an external alignment server, which the team had not approved. A single-sequence run (no alignment, so nothing was sent to an external server) was made on 17 October 2026 on one GPU of the iGEM server: Boltz-2 on the 1F91 construct (E. coli KAS I, C163S) with decanoic acid, five models, seed 42, with a soft 8 Å contact on S163 and without. Ligand-carbon RMSD to the crystal decanoate (over four crystal copies, after fitting the Cα atoms near the ligand; chain matched from the carboxyl carbon) was 4.8–5.2 Å for the best constrained model and 8–15 Å for the others (median about 10 Å), and 8.1–12.3 Å unconstrained; overall confidence was only 0.33–0.37 and ligand ipTM 0.44–0.61. By the pre-registered criterion (above 4 Å makes pose analysis descriptive only) this control was not passed. Because a single-sequence prediction is weaker than the alignment-based pipeline used on the target (and the 1F91 entry is probably in the training data), the result shows that this control configuration does not recover the known pose, not that the main pipeline would fail; a control with a proper alignment is still needed. The only positive control that was run, Vina docking on 1F91, failed (Engineering, cycle 2).
  • Per-residue confidence (pLDDT, PAE) around the pocket, pairwise ligand and backbone RMSD across all models and a consensus rule were not computed; only model-level scores are reported.

Reproducing the Boltz-2 runs

In brief

The software, inputs, commands and measurements needed to repeat the runs and recompute the reported distances and scores are listed here.

Software and hardware. Rounds 2 to 6 were run with Boltz-21 (Python package boltz 2.2.1 with the Boltz-2 weights) on Linux with NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs (48 GB), one GPU per run. Round 1 used Google Colab with a T4 GPU. A run with five samples took about 7–9 min on an idle GPU, 13–15 min on a shared one and, in round 6 on a heavily loaded server, 20–30 min (some runs did not finish). Python, PyTorch and CUDA versions and the weights hash were not recorded. The output of one run (structures and confidence files) occupied about 13 MB.

Input. The protein was residues 1123–1657 of S. cerevisiae FAS2 (UniProt P19097; 535 residues). Residue indices in the inputs are local: UniProt number minus 1122, so that M1251, V1254, M1260 and C1305 are residues 129, 132, 138 and 183. The ligand was given as a SMILES string (myristic acid, CCCCCCCCCCCCCC(=O)O; C8 CCCCCCCC(=O)O; C16 and C22 by extending the chain; the decoys caffeine Cn1cnc2c1c(=O)n(C)c(=O)n2C and glucose OC[C@H]1OC(O)[C@H](O)[C@@H](O)[C@@H]1O). Variants were made by substituting the residue in the input sequence: V1254W and V1254A at residue 132, M1251W at residue 129, and the plug mutant at residues 129, 182, 226 and 426 (M1251W, A1304W, A1348W, A1548W). The constraint designs were a soft contact on C1305 (contacts: [[A, 183]], max_distance: 8.0; 6 or 10 in the sweep) and forced contacts on M1251 and M1260 (contacts: [[A, 129], [A, 138]], with residue 132 added in one run, and force: true). The binding-probability output was requested with a properties block.

Alignments. Round 2 used the public alignment server of Boltz (--use_msa_server). Rounds 3 and 4 read a fixed alignment file (msa: field) generated for the wild-type sequence in round 2. For variants, the first row of that file must be the variant sequence: in the installed version, an alignment whose first row differed from the input sequence was replaced by a single-sequence placeholder.

Up to three runs were executed concurrently, one per GPU; a run took 7–9 min on an otherwise idle GPU (the earlier figure of about 7 min was for the round-2 setup without the affinity stage).

Measurements. For each of the five models of a run, the closest heavy-atom distance between the ligand and each residue of interest (local residues 129, 132, 138 and 183) and the closest ligand–protein atom pair were computed from the structure file. Overall confidence (confidence_score), ligand ipTM (ligand_iptm) and complex ipLDDT (complex_iplddt) were read from the confidence_*.json files. Ligand displacement was the distance between the centroids of the ligand heavy atoms after superposing the protein Cα atoms on the reference model. Medians over the five models are reported unless stated.

Expected agreement. With the same seed, alignment, settings and software on the same server, a rerun reproduced the stored wild-type models exactly (matched-model ligand RMSD 0.00 Å). A different seed did not reproduce the poses (median matched-model ligand RMSD 2.14 Å), so comparisons between runs require the same seed or a distribution over several seeds.
