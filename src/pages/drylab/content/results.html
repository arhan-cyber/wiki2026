Summary of findings

In brief

In the computer models, the theoretical maximum of free myristic acid export is non-zero in unedited yeast, but only by shortening a longer fatty acid, and no gene deletion set tested improved on the unedited strain. The pre-flight checks showed that the team’s scoring setup could not support a ranking. The models were built to guide the wet-lab V1254W construct, and its measurements will be set against the predictions reported here.

  1. In the model, C14:0 export has an upper bound that falls linearly with the growth required. This is the maximum when C14:0 is the only objective (purity is 100 % because only C14:0 is maximised), not a titre prediction. None of the 175 knockout sets of up to three of ten named genes improved on wild type, with or without the early-release reaction. Details
  2. Without early release, the model carried C14:0 through one route at the optimum: shortening C16:0 through β-oxidation. POX1 catalyses the committed step. Blocking one step switches to another route. Details
  3. A fatty acid synthase (FAS) early-release reaction raised C14:0 export by 2.6 %. The limit was NADPH, not chain supply. Details
  4. The ScFAS2 residue equivalent to the Yarrowia chain-length residue is V1254. The structural step did not test V1254W; M1251 was deprioritised as a precaution although published S. cerevisiae M1251W strains are productive; the effect of V1254W in S. cerevisiae is the assumption that the wet-lab construct tests. Details
  5. The pre-flight checks showed that the scoring setup could not support a ranking. The best knockout set exceeded the unedited strain by 0.010 %, so the tool issued no proposals and effort went into the single mutation. Details
  6. One learned search agent was outperformed by simpler searches on one synthetic landscape. Brute force and greedy search outperformed a linear Q-learning agent. Details
  7. Methyl myristate wins in use and loses on production. It emits 17 % less CO₂ per server-year than an ester-based coolant, but making 1 kg emits far more. Details

Reachable C14:0 under growth constraints

In brief

In the model, the maximum output of myristic acid falls in a straight line as more growth is required, from 0.211 at 10 % of normal growth to 0.024 at 90 % (mmol per gram dry weight per hour). No gene deletion set tested improved on this.

Metabolic route to C14:0

In brief

At the optimum, the model makes myristic acid by one route: chopping two carbons off palmitic acid, with the gene POX1 controlling the key step. Deleting POX1 removed all output.

At the optimum, C14:0 was carried by one route: C16:0 → hexadecanoyl-CoA → acyl-CoA oxidase (POX1) → enoyl-CoA hydratase → myristoyl-CoA → free C14:0 → export. Flux variability analysis at the maximum found no alternative route with the same C14 export (each step varies by less than 10⁻⁵), although blocking one step (r_0102) shifts the model to a C18:1 → C12 → C14 route at a lower export. Deletion of POX1 gave exactly zero. Loopless flux balance analysis (FBA) and a forced one-way ligase changed the result by at most 2 %.

Changes to the plan

In brief

Four decisions followed from the models: a planned gene-deletion strategy was dropped, a new reaction was added to the model, five residues were set aside as mutation sites on literature grounds (only one firmly), and no ranked list of deletions went to the laboratory.

  • The plan to knock down degradation to preserve C14:0 was dropped, because degradation is the only C14:0 source in the absence of early release.
  • A FAS early-release reaction was added as a documented model modification: C14 +2.6 % at 50 % growth, limited by NADPH (Figure 2).
  • C1305 was excluded from mutagenesis as the catalytic nucleophile; M1251, F1646, G1250 and K1413–K1423 were deprioritised as a precaution (a literature-based choice; published S. cerevisiae G1250S and G1250S-M1251W strains show these positions tolerate substitution and give C6/C8 products). V1254W was retained as the lead candidate; if the homology assumption is wrong, the published S. cerevisiae variants point to C6/C8, the wrong product.
  • The wet lab was given one design, the single mutation: a ranked knockout list was withheld because checks D1 and D2 showed the scoring could not support it.

Structural result: site and pocket

In brief

The matching position in baker’s yeast for the Yarrowia chain-length residue is V1254. An early structure prediction suggested a collision with another residue, M1251, but repeat runs did not show it, so no collision is claimed. The structure predictions did not separate the fatty acid from decoy molecules and varied with the random seed as much as with the substitutions tested.

V1254 (ScFAS2) corresponds to I1220 (YlFAS), with a 34-residue offset and no gaps at or beside the key column. In the first constrained Boltz-2 runs, C14 was positioned 4.35 Å from V1254 and 6.91 Å from M1260 and overlapped M1251 at 0.96–1.77 Å, which was interpreted as a closed conformational gate. Reruns on the laboratory server did not reproduce the overlap, and distances varied with the choice of constrained residues, so these numbers describe the setup as well as the pocket. Details

Life cycle assessment headline

In brief

A screening life cycle assessment finds that, in use, the candidate coolant emits 17 % less carbon per server-year than an ester-based coolant, while making it emits far more per kilogram. The study has not been independently reviewed.

In this screening-level study, methyl myristate emitted 5,012 kg CO₂-eq per server-year in use, against 6,071 kg CO₂-eq for an ester-based coolant (−17 %), at a derived ecoinvent India grid factor of about 1.6 kg CO₂-eq kWh⁻¹. The advantage comes from lower cooling electricity: methyl myristate meets 85 °C at 0.27 m s⁻¹ against 0.80 m s⁻¹, which cuts pump power from 158.9 W to 6.1 W. Production per kg was higher for methyl myristate (449 against about 5 kg CO₂-eq kg⁻¹), paid back in about 2.9 years in the base case. On the cleaner CEA grid factor (0.716 kg CO₂-eq kWh⁻¹) the optimised process emits 14 % more than the ester-based coolant, and payback (6.6 years) exceeds the assumed 5-year coolant life; the steps 1–3 process (about 270 kg CO₂-eq kg⁻¹) stays 9 % ahead. The study has not undergone an ISO 14044 critical review. Life Cycle Assessment

Lessons and design changes

In brief

Early checks refined the design: a ranking with almost no margin led to the pre-flight tool, a setup-dependent structure prediction led to a standard of five samples and a second seed, and a simple loop proved as good as a learning method.

  • The first ranking appeared authoritative but carried no information (margin 0.010 %). The relevant threshold is the resolution of the assay, not the tolerance of the solver. Change caused: the pre-flight tool and the refusal to issue proposals.
  • Unconstrained Boltz-2 placed the fatty acid poorly in the first run but near the catalytic cysteine in a rerun, so no claim is made either way. Change caused: five samples and a second seed became the minimum.
  • Three standard remedies (UFF minimisation, sculpting, mutagenesis wizard) did not resolve the M1251 overlap seen in the first runs; the reruns did not show it.
  • A reinforcement-learning agent was outperformed by a short greedy loop. Change caused: brute force is recommended at this scale (see Trust). Every cycle and what it changed is in the Engineering register of failures.
  • The metabolic model could not represent the intended intervention until a reaction was added by hand.

Further work

In brief

The natural extensions are to repeat the main analyses with the early-release reaction, extend growth costs to multi-gene sets, add structural controls, and compare the model with measured fatty-acid profiles.

Natural extensions of this work: repeat the frontier with the early-release reaction; add multi-gene growth penalties; add the alignment-based structural positive control, unrestrained simulation and more seeds, and report per-residue confidence; and compare the model with published and measured fatty-acid profiles, including those of the wet-lab strain.
