Every number on the model pages is a model output. The wet-lab team built the V1254W FAS2 construct that these models point to, and its measurements are set against the predictions (see Integration).

Overview of the modelling chain

In brief

The project asks whether baker’s yeast could be engineered to make myristic acid, a 14-carbon fatty acid, for use in a bio-based data-centre coolant. Five linked modelling steps and two downstream analyses (CFD and LCA) were built. The central assumption, that a single mutation (V1254W) releases this fatty acid early, is the one tested by the wet-lab construct.

  • The synthetic-biology work was organised in five steps, each passing one result to the next: sequence → fatty acid synthase (FAS) interaction → bridge → metabolism → trust, followed by two downstream analyses of the product as a coolant (CFD and LCA, each on its own tab) (figure on the home page; its sixth card, ‘Process’, groups the two downstream analyses).
  • Central assumption: a point mutation in Saccharomyces cerevisiae FAS2 (V1254W, by analogy with Yarrowia lipolytica I1220W) can release C14 early. This is assumption A1, and the wet-lab V1254W strain is its experimental test.
  • Main findings: in the model without early release, C14:0 is formed only by shortening C16:0 through β-oxidation; none of the 175 knockout sets of up to three of ten named genes exceeded wild type, with or without the early-release reaction; an early-release reaction increased C14 export by only 2.6 % and is limited by NADPH; the team’s scoring setup did not satisfy its own checks. These are properties of one model under stated conditions, not predictions of a strain.

Approach and outputs of each step

In brief

Each step answers one question with one method: a sequence comparison, a protein-ligand prediction, a metabolic-model extension, a flux calculation, checks on the ranking, and heat-flow and life cycle assessments. Flux balance analysis gives upper limits, not predictions.

Table 1. Steps of the modelling chain, the question addressed, the method and the output of each.

Step Question Method Output
1 Sequence Which ScFAS2 residue corresponds to the Yarrowia chain-length residue? EMBOSS Needle global alignment A candidate site (V1254W)
2 FAS interaction Does the ketoacyl synthase (KS) pocket accommodate a C14 chain; which residues must not be mutated? Boltz-2 on the KS domain with distance constraints; structural comparison Pose descriptions that depend on the setup; no result that separates C14 from decoy ligands or substitutions from seed noise (see the Answer on the step page)
3 Bridge Can a metabolic model represent the mutation? Addition of an early-release reaction to Yeast9 A model that can simulate the intervention
4 Metabolism What can the cell reach, and at what growth cost? Flux balance analysis (FBA) on Yeast9, COBRApy Feasibility frontier, route, knockout screen
5 Trust Does the top-ranked design differ from the unedited strain? Is learned search required? Six checks on the scoring function; brute force, greedy and reinforcement-learning search Rankings that may be relied on
CFD and LCA (separate tabs) Is the product suitable for its intended use? Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) of an immersion tank; screening life cycle assessment (LCA) Lower pump power and CO₂ in use; higher production footprint per kg

Rationale for the methods. FBA1 requires no kinetic parameters and can therefore be applied to a genome-scale model; it yields an upper limit and not a prediction. Boltz-22 was used because the KS pocket of the solved ScFAS2 structure (PDB 8PRW)3 contains no fatty acid, so ligand placement had to be predicted. Brute force was retained as the reference search because it is exact and inexpensive at the present scale. Existing strain-design tools, namely OptKnock,4 OptGene,5 OptForce,6 RobustKnock,7 cameo,8 MEWpy,9 OptFlux10 and StrainDesign,11 return designs whether or not the underlying scoring can separate candidates. In the team’s reading of the papers and documentation of these eight tools, none was found to run a null-intervention or measurement-resolution check on its scoring before emitting designs. No survey table (versions, documents read, search terms) was kept, so this is a statement of what the team did not find, not a proof that no such check exists.

Data and parameters

In brief

Protein structures come from public databases, and the metabolic model is the community yeast model Yeast9. Two flux analyses were run under different conditions. Their C14 exports differ by 6–7 % and their wild-type growth rates by 20 %; a single-parameter check (below) shows that glucose uptake explains about half of the growth difference.

Literature inputs

  • Chain-length control by the KS domain and the Yarrowia I1220W result: Rigouin et al. (2017), in which C14 reached 11.6 % of total fatty acids.13
  • Catalytic and gating residues of ScFAS: Johansson et al. (2008), PDB 2VKZ.12
  • Background fatty-acid profile of S. cerevisiae (CEN.PK113-7D, nitrogen-limited medium, harvested after 120 h; per cent of total fatty acids, n = 2): C16:0 13.7, C16:1 50.0, C18:0 3.5, C18:1 30.0.14 This profile is from a nitrogen-limited, stationary-phase culture, whereas the flux balance analyses assume balanced growth at a fixed glucose uptake; the mismatch is not resolved, and the profile is used only as an illustrative background in Step 5.
  • Assay noise: replicate variation of GC-MS fatty-acid composition assumed to be 1–5 % (relative). No literature source or team replicate data are cited for this range; it is taken as an assumption, and the thresholds of checks D1 and D2 scale with it.

Assumptions and their basis

In brief

Eleven assumptions underlie the results. The most important, A1, is that the mutation works in baker’s yeast as it does in Yarrowia; if it does not, every downstream number is moot. Published work found by the team reports shorter chains (C6 and C8) instead.

Effect of the results on the design

In brief

The models changed the plan in six ways. A plan to reduce fat breakdown was dropped, some residues were deprioritised as a precaution, and no ranked list of gene deletions was sent to the laboratory. Choosing between two candidate coolants is still open.

  1. The plan to knock down degradation in order to preserve C14:0 was dropped. The model indicates that degradation is the only C14:0 source at the optimum in the absence of early release, and deletion of POX1 gave zero (with early release on, POX1 deletion no longer abolishes C14, see Step 4). The rule against sending degradation knockdowns to the wet lab in a first iteration therefore has a mechanistic basis; the first wet-lab iteration is a single FAS2 mutation.
  2. An FAS early-release reaction was added as a documented model modification, and its effect was measured: +2.6 %, NADPH-limited. This indicates where the model places the limit (cofactors, not chain supply). The next wet-lab design step can use this result to target NADPH supply.
  3. C1305 was excluded from mutagenesis as the catalytic nucleophile. M1251, F1646, G1250 and K1413–K1423 were deprioritised as a precaution (a literature-based choice; published S. cerevisiae G1250S and G1250S-M1251W strains show that these positions tolerate substitution and give C6/C8, so the deprioritisation is a precaution and not a finding about the enzyme). V1254W was retained as the lead candidate, with M1260 secondary. Step 2 did not test this choice; the wet-lab construct does.
  4. The wet lab therefore received one design, the single mutation: checks D1 and D2 showed that the knockout scoring could not support a ranked list, so the tool withheld proposals, and effort went into the V1254W route.
  5. Brute-force enumeration is recommended at the present scale, together with a trivial baseline before any learned search.

Validation. The models are compared with literature where a published result exists, and with the wet-lab V1254W strain as measurements become available. The checks run so far:

  • Negative controls for the structural step: caffeine and glucose decoys were run (Step 2, Table 5) and were not separated from C14 by the confidence scores, so the structural confidence scores are not used as evidence of binding.
  • Positive controls for the structural step: Vina docking on 1F91 and a single-sequence Boltz-2 run on 1F91 (best 4.8 Å) did not recover the crystal pose; an alignment-based Boltz-2 control on 1F91 is the natural next check (see Table 5).
  • Metabolic model: a comparison with the published Yarrowia C14 shift and the S. cerevisiae C6/C8 shifts requires C6/C8 release reactions, which the model does not contain, so no claim of agreement with published outcomes is made.
  • Per-residue confidence for the final models is not extracted; model-level scores are reported.

Terms used on these pages

In brief

Short definitions of the technical terms that appear with numbers on the step pages.

  • ipTM and ligand ipTM. Boltz-2’s predicted interface score (0 to 1) for the pairing of chains; the ligand version is computed for the ligand–protein interface. Higher means the model is more confident; it is a self-assessment, not agreement with an experiment.
  • Complex ipLDDT and interface position error. Boltz-2 confidence outputs for the interface region: a local-confidence score (0 to 1) and a predicted distance error in Ångström (lower is better).
  • Steering potentials. Physics-based terms that Boltz-2 can apply during structure generation (the --use_potentials option) to discourage clashes and to satisfy constraints.
  • cealign. The PyMOL command for CE structural alignment, used to superimpose two protein structures and report the RMSD.
  • NGAM. Non-growth-associated maintenance: the ATP the model must spend per gram of cells per hour even without growth (0.7 mmol here).
  • GLPK. The GNU Linear Programming Kit, the solver used for the flux balance problems.
  • Loopless FBA. Flux balance analysis with thermodynamically impossible circular flux loops removed.
  • Tie band. The range of loss values that cannot be told apart from each other at the assumed measurement noise.
  • Reaction identifiers (r_0402, r_0123, r_2111, r_2193 and so on). Reaction numbers in the Yeast-GEM model: r_2111 is the biomass reaction, r_2193 the myristate exchange, r_0402 the step forming hexadecanoyl-CoA from C16:0, r_0123 acyl-CoA oxidase, r_2251 enoyl-CoA hydratase, r_0102 the myristoyl-CoA step and r_0412 the acyl-CoA to free-acid step.
  • Yeast9 and Yeast-GEM v9.1.1. Yeast9 is the community consensus model of S. cerevisiae; v9.1.1 is the release of it used for every result here.

In brief

The checking tool is open source (MIT licence) and two example scripts reproduce its main outputs. The expected output of the diagnostics script is a refusal to issue proposals, which is the demonstration and not a fault.
