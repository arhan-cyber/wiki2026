Step 4. Whole-cell metabolic analysis

In brief

Flux balance analysis, a way of calculating the most a cell’s metabolism could produce, was applied to a genome-scale yeast model. Without the early-release reaction the model makes myristic acid only by shortening longer fatty acids at the optimum, the maximum output falls steadily as growth is required, and none of the 175 knockout sets tested beat the unedited strain, with or without the early-release reaction. [computed]

Question. Which quantity of C14:0 can the cell reach in the model, at what cost to growth, and by which route? The analyses used flux balance analysis (FBA)1 on Yeast92 with COBRApy3. Conditions of analysis B are given in the Data section of the model overview.

Formulation. Every result below is the solution of a linear program: maximise the flux vi of the chosen objective reaction (r_2193, myristate export, unless stated) subject to S v = 0 (steady state, S the stoichiometric matrix of Yeast9), lower and upper flux bounds on every reaction (glucose uptake r_1714 at least −1.0 mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹ in analysis A, −1.1 in B; non-growth-associated maintenance r_4046 fixed at 0.7), and a growth requirement vr_2111 ≥ f · μWT with f the fraction of wild-type growth. A gene deletion sets the flux of every reaction that needs the gene to zero.

Feasibility frontier (analysis A)

In brief

In the unedited model, pure myristic acid can be exported at any growth up to 90 % of normal, and output falls in a straight line as more growth is required: 0.211 at 10 % growth to 0.024 at 90 % (mmol per gram dry weight per hour). [computed]

[computed] In unmodified Yeast9, the wild-type model exported C14:0 at any growth up to 90 % of the wild-type rate without genetic edits when C14:0 export was the only objective. Purity is 100 % here because only C14:0 is maximised; it is a property of the objective and not a prediction of a strain, and these values are theoretical upper bounds and not titre predictions (no membrane fatty-acid requirement beyond the biomass reaction, no regulation). Growth traded off linearly against yield (Table 1, Figure 1).

Metabolic route to C14:0

In brief

Without the added reaction, the model makes myristic acid only by chopping two carbons off palmitic acid (C16:0) in the peroxisome, so deleting the gene POX1 removes it completely. The original plan of reducing breakdown is therefore inverted relative to what the model predicts. [computed]

[computed] At 50 % growth every step of one route carried identical flux (0.1192 mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹). Identical flux alone would not show that no alternative route exists, so flux variability analysis was run at the maximum C14 export (17 October 2026): every step of the route varies by less than 10⁻⁵ mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹, so at this optimum no alternative route carries the same export. Blocking one step (r_0102) does open another route at a lower export (below). The route is therefore the only one at the optimum, not a proof that the network has no other:

C16:0 → (r_0402) hexadecanoyl-CoA → (r_0123, acyl-CoA oxidase, POX1) → (r_2251) enoyl-CoA hydratase → (r_0102) myristoyl-CoA → (r_0412) free C14:0 → export (Figure B).

The model formed C14:0 by removing one two-carbon unit from C16:0 through β-oxidation, not by termination of synthesis. POX1 catalyses the committed step, and its deletion therefore gave exactly zero. Robustness checks: forbidding reversal of the ATP-driven ligase step lowered production by 2 % (0.1192 to 0.1167 mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹), and loopless FBA returned identical flux. Blocking r_0102 (analysis B) lowered export at the 50 % growth floor from 0.12719 to 0.10977 mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹, and the model switched to an alternative route (degradation of C18:1 to C12 followed by re-elongation); blocking both reactions abolished C14 export. With the early-release reaction on, the route changes: deleting POX1, PXA1 or PXA2 no longer reduces C14 export (Table 3), because the early-release reaction supplies the C14; the degradation-pathway result therefore holds only for the model without early release.

The original plan was to reduce degradation in order to preserve C14:0. In this model, degradation is the only source of C14:0 at the optimum in the absence of the early-release reaction, so the strategy is inverted relative to the model prediction.

Knockout and blocking screen (analysis B)

In brief

All 1,143 single-gene deletions were screened in the model. None forced myristic acid export, and removing genes for peroxisomal fat breakdown abolished it. [computed]

[computed]

  • All 1,143 single-gene deletions were screened in both ligase scenarios: 965 were viable and 178 lethal (lethal denotes no positive biomass flux, a computational classification and not a prediction of cell death; the numerical threshold of the original analysis was not recorded). None forced myristate export at the maximum growth rate of the deleted strain. A recomputation under analysis A conditions (17 October 2026; threshold 10⁻⁹ h⁻¹) gave 954 viable and 189 lethal; two genes (YDR044W, YJL101C) hit a 20 s solver limit and are counted as lethal. The difference from analysis B (11 genes) is plausibly the BY4742 supplements of analysis B, which was not tested.
  • Deletion of POX1, FOX2, POT1, PXA1 or PXA2 abolished modelled C14 export, because the C14-producing routes depend on peroxisomal β-oxidation. Deletions of FAS1, FAS2, ACC1 and OLE1 were lethal in the specified medium.
  • Blocking individual C14-consuming reactions (r_0125, r_0105, r_2155, r_2195/r_2201, r_2220) changed nothing at 50 % growth, because all carried zero flux at that optimum.

Scope of the combination screen. The 175 combinations are all sets of one, two or three of ten candidate genes chosen by the team in fatty-acid degradation (POX1, PXA1, PXA2), activation (FAA1, FAA4), storage (DGA1, LRO1, ARE1, ARE2) and desaturation (OLE1). The statement that no knockout set exceeded wild type therefore means: none of these 175 sets exceeded wild-type C14 export at 50 % of wild-type growth. It does not cover other genes, four or more knockouts, overexpression, or strain-design algorithms (OptKnock-type searches) that were not run on this model.

Consistency checks

In brief

Two ways of writing the cell’s growth in the model gave identical results, so one was used throughout. [computed]

[computed] The biomass reaction r_2111 and the biomass pseudo-reaction r_4041 carried identical flux in every solution (the biomass metabolite has exactly one producer and one consumer), so either gives the same results. Reaction r_2111 was used throughout, being the default objective of the model.

Growth-rate penalty

In brief

The growth penalty of each single-gene deletion has been calculated (below); multi-gene sets and early-release strains have not. [computed]

The penalty of an intervention is defined as (1 − μmax,i / μmax,WT) × 100, where μmax,WT is the maximum growth rate of the unmodified model and μmax,i that after intervention i. For all 1,143 single-gene deletions under analysis A conditions (wild type 0.0811 h⁻¹; computed 17 October 2026): 954 deletions are viable, and of these 893 have a penalty below 0.1 %, 43 a penalty above 1 % and 31 above 10 %; 189 are lethal. Deletion of POX1, PXA1, PXA2, FAA1, FAA4, DGA1, LRO1, ARE1 and ARE2 each has a penalty of 0.0 %; OLE1, FAS1 and FAS2 are lethal. The knockout sets of Table 3 carry no growth cost beyond those that cannot reach the 50 % floor (54 sets, for example ARE1 with ARE2). The penalty of strains carrying the early-release reaction is not defined, because that reaction is not a deletion; the 2.6 % gain in export is at a fixed 50 % growth. [computed]
