Goal of the study

This study quantifies the cradle-to-gate environmental impacts of methyl myristate (MM) made by engineered Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and compares it with commercial immersion coolants. It also tests how MM's thermal performance in a server tank changes that comparison.

1.1 Context

Air cooling is reaching its limits as data-centre and HPC power density rises, and single-phase immersion cooling is a leading alternative. The incumbent dielectric fluids each carry a liability:

  • Fluorinated fluids (PFAS) face phase-out under the proposed EU universal PFAS restriction because of extreme persistence and high GWP.
  • Ester-based coolants and mineral-oil-based coolants (refined mineral oils and synthetic hydrocarbon grades) are made largely from fossil feedstocks: crude-oil refining or ethylene oligomerisation for the mineral-oil-based fluids, petrochemical or palm-derived acids and polyols for the esters.

1.2 Objectives

  1. Comparative assessment: quantify MM's impacts against two commercial coolant families, ester-based and mineral-oil-based, using the same database, method and level of process detail.
  2. Hotspot identification: find which fermentation and downstream steps (centrifugation, cell disruption, solvent extraction, distillation, drying) drive MM's impacts, to guide strain and process design.
  3. Decarbonisation pathway: test what productivity, energy integration and renewable energy MM needs to match the incumbents, instead of assuming that it does.

1.3 Audience

  • Primary: the iGEM 2026 judging panel and the synthetic-biology community.
  • Secondary: data-centre thermal engineers, sustainability leads and bioprocess developers.

The study is not intended for public comparative assertions under ISO 14044 §5.2 without critical review.

Functional units and reference flows

The study uses two functional units: one for production and one for the cooling service the fluid delivers. Per-kg results alone ignore the fact that the fluids need different flow rates and pumping energy to cool the same server.

FU-1

Production

Definition
1 kg of finished coolant at the plant gate
Boundary
Cradle-to-gate
Reference flow
1 kg of coolant (MM purity 98.5%)
Used for
Hotspots, all 16 EF 3.1 categories
FU-2

Cooling service

Definition
Cooling one 1,200 W server for 1 year (8,760 h), hottest component ≤ 85 °C, coolant supply at 25 °C
Boundary
Cradle-to-gate production + use-phase cooling electricity
Reference flow
Coolant per year = tank fill (L) × density ÷ service life, plus cooling electricity (kWh/yr) at the lowest flow that meets 85 °C
Used for
Climate change, pPUE, break-even

FU-2 follows the performance-based approach recommended in ISO 14044 §4.2.3.2, where products are compared on the function they deliver. PUE itself is a ratio, not a quantity of service, so it is reported as a result (partial PUE, pPUE) rather than used as the functional unit. The server's own IT electricity (10,512 kWh/yr) is the same for every coolant and is excluded from FU-2.

Summary

In use, methyl myristate (MM) has a lower carbon footprint than commercial coolants. Cooling one 1.2 kW server for a year with MM emits 5,012 kg CO₂-eq vs 6,071 kg with an ester-based coolant (−17%), and −25% with free cooling; with the planned process steps this grows to −28% and −46%. MM costs more CO₂ to make per kg, but a server needs only ~3.4 kg of coolant a year, while MM saves ~1,600 kWh of cooling electricity in that year.

openLCA per-kg results [27] + team OpenFOAM CFD [28]; same 20 L fill and 5-year life for every coolant; §7.4 base case.

  • Per kg (cradle-to-gate): MM baseline 632, MM optimised process 449 (openLCA, 14 Oct), MM with process steps 1–3 ~270 (estimate). Commercial: ester-based coolants 5.17–5.25, mineral-oil-based coolants ~1.5–3.00 (refined grade to synthetic grade).
  • Per server-year (1.2 kW server, one consistent grid factor): MM needs one third of the flow and 26× less pump power than the ester-based coolant. That saves ~1,600 kWh a year, so MM's optimised process emits 5,012 kg CO₂-eq vs 6,071 for the ester-based coolant (−17%), and −25% with free cooling (§7.4; tank results from the team's OpenFOAM CFD simulations, see the CFD tab).
  • Where MM wins outright: 9.9 °C cooler at equal flow, pPUE 1.035, 93% biogenic carbon, 17× less fossil CO₂ at end of life than the mineral-oil-based coolants, 33% lower land-use-change CO₂ than the ester-based coolants, PFAS-free (§4.2) and readily biodegradable.
